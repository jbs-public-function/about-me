# Professional Biography & Executive Narrative

## James Barrett
*Senior Computational Scientist & Cloud Infrastructure Engineer*  
*Specializing in Cloud Optimization, MLOps, and High-Performance APIs*

---

## 1. Who I Am

I approach software engineering with the mindset of a night-shift janitor: identifying chaotic, unoptimized structures, establishing clear-cut logical pathways, and leaving the platform cleaner, faster, and more cost-effective than I found it. 

Transitioning from physical janitorial work to systems architecture, I developed a unique perspective on efficiency by applying Big O notation to cleaning routes to optimize shifts, later translating that exact spatial/operational optimization to cloud infrastructure. Today, I specialize in reducing complex cloud spend (e.g., slashing VPC transfer costs from $800/day to ~$0/day), architecting low-latency gRPC microservices, and deploying enterprise-scale MLOps stacks (such as Metaflow via Terraform) to handle massive, multi-terabyte data streams.

---

## 2. The Arc of My Career: "From Janitor to Data Janitor"

### The Starting Point
I graduated high school without much direction. I wasn't a strong student, and college felt out of reach. I wandered through various survival jobs: fry cook, gas station attendant, and FedEx Ground package handler. Eventually, I secured a job with a local janitorial company. 

For a directionless person, janitorial work was fascinating. It was clear-cut, tactile, and had immediate feedback loops. The building isn't clean; by the end of your shift, it will be. Empty the trash, wipe down desks, squeegee the glass, clean the bathrooms, dust, vacuum, mop. Done. I took pride in doing it well. Within a year, I could complete an 8-hour shift in 5 to 6 hours.

### Unlocking the Mind
To satisfy my growing curiosity, I enrolled in Parkland Community College. I had residual math anxiety, testing into pre-college algebra. There, a phenomenal math department unlocked something in me. I spent hours at a time doing every odd problem in my textbooks, drilling until algebra became rote. Confidence followed. 

After graduating with an Associate Degree in Business Administration, I transferred to the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign (UIUC). I began playing with Lua (Minecraft scripting) and VBA for Excel, realizing I loved programming.

### UIUC & The Power of Big O
At UIUC, I majored in Economics to learn how to solve econometric questions with computing, while heavily loading my schedule with advanced mathematics: discrete math, linear algebra, and calculus. I also pushed into core Computer Science courses, most notably **CS 225: Data Structures & Algorithms** (implementing complex structures in C++ with manual memory management).

Throughout college, I maintained a grueling schedule:
* **Day (9:00 AM - 3:00 PM):** Full-time coursework at UIUC.
* **Night (5:00 PM - 1:30 AM):** Night-shift janitor, Monday through Friday, plus overtime, to support myself and my daughter.

During this time, Donald Knuth and Big O notation heavily influenced me. I actively applied time complexity to my cleaning routes. *Is it faster to clean a whole room and then move to the next? Is it more efficient to commit to one task at a time across the entire floor?* Walking and cleaning became my mental runtime for thinking through calculus and C++ memory management bugs.

### The Breakthrough
Upon graduating, I was hired in 2017 as a Data Science Intern at Agrible, Inc., an ag-tech startup in UIUC's Research Park. The day I was hired, the company laid off half its staff due to funding turbulence. I stayed focused, resolved to make my mark, and threw myself into the engineering codebase. 

My first breakthrough was refactoring a complex, multi-module agricultural simulation model from Python 2.7 to 3.6. Naively accepting the massive challenge, I successfully migrated it and optimized its memory footprint, reducing the execution loop from a full minute down to 5 seconds. I became hooked on optimization.

In 2018, I was hired full-time, and later that year, Agrible was acquired by Nutrien, Inc. My career shifted completely toward **Infrastructure Engineering, Systems Architecture, and MLOps**, where I have spent the last several years architecting planetary-scale data streams and slashing cloud overhead.

## 3. Core Philosophy & Value Proposition

My career transition has instilled three guiding principles that define my technical leadership:
1. **Ruthless Cost & Performance Optimization:** If a query can run in 5 seconds instead of 60, or if a data cache can eliminate an $800/day VPC bill, it is my responsibility to build it. Every redundant byte has a financial cost.
2. **Simplification of the Complex:** Just as janitorial systems rely on standardized, repeatable patterns to maintain a massive facility, complex software systems must be simplified through clear-cut automation (e.g., Terraform, Serverless Batch, and Step Functions).
3. **Resiliency Under Pressure:** Balancing full-time UIUC coursework with 40+ hours of night-shift cleaning taught me work ethic and focus. I do not panic when systems break; I isolate the issue, draft a clear plan of action, and sweep the floor until it's spotless.
